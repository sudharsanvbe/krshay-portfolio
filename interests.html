Why the bat, the track and the dojo

Cricket is where the interest in motion started — the wind-up, the release, the split-second read of a length. Middle-distance running turned that instinct into a measurable habit: pacing, breath, recovery. Karate added the discipline underneath both — the black belt didn't come from talent, it came from showing up on the days motivation didn't.

Why it stuck with me

A masterclass in strategy, patience and thinking several moves ahead — it showed how engineering, people and decisions come together under pressure, one cool head at a time.

  • Information is power, but clarity is more powerful.

What travel means, so far

Travel, for me, has mostly happened through sport — a passport that fills up one start line at a time rather than one holiday at a time. Bahrain was the first stamp that came from competing rather than sightseeing, and it changed how I think about travel: not a break from the training, but an extension of it.

Every Nationals trip adds a city I wouldn't otherwise have seen — a new track, a new crowd, a new way competitors from other states train. That's the part of travel I didn't expect to enjoy as much as the racing itself: comparing notes.
